1. Do the late games become seed races?

Fans pay more attention when a game can change the playoff picture. This counts how often that happens in the final third of the season.

Seed-race game: a game in the last third of the season between two teams from the same conference who are within 1 game of each other in the standings. Example: the 5th and 6th seeds, half a game apart, play each other in April.

2. What does each schedule cost the owners?

Fewer games means fewer tickets to sell. The matched finish keeps all 82 games, so its only cost is the NBA Cup money it displaces. Figures are per team, per year, after the players' share.

This is a simplified conference with made-up team strengths, not real ratings. Each round every team plays once, either a conference game or a cross-conference game against an average opponent. It uses the same home-court edge (2 points) and game-to-game randomness (13.5 points) as the main study, and its averages land close to the study's numbers in section 1.

What this does not show

Whether closer races earn more money. The case for the matched finish rests on a break-even calculation, not on evidence that those games draw bigger audiences.

The Cup's real value. It is not public. Move the Cup sliders to see how much the answer depends on it.

Everything that makes a game matter besides the standings. Stars, rivalries and storylines are not measured.

Fairness. Matching by standings gives the top seeds a harder finish than the bottom seeds.
